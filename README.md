📊Country-Wise COVID-19 Dashboard — Overview

This dashboard visualizes global COVID-19 data aggregated by WHO regions. It provides quick insights into case severity, recovery progress, and geographical spread.

**Key Metrics**

1. 16M+ Confirmed Cases — Total reported infections.
2. 654K Deaths — Cumulative global fatalities.
3. 9M Recovered — Total recovered patients.
4. 6M Active Cases — Ongoing infections (Confirmed − Recovered − Deaths).

These KPIs provide a snapshot of the pandemic’s overall status.

**Deaths vs. Recoveries (by WHO Region)**
The first bar chart compares the number of deaths and recoveries:

1. Europe and the Americas show the highest case outcomes, with both large recovery counts and high deaths.
2. Africa and South-East Asia have lower totals, but recoveries still exceed deaths.
3. Overall, most regions show stronger recovery trends than fatality rates.

**New Cases, New Deaths & New Recoveries**
The second bar chart highlights recent pandemic activity:

1. Europe and Africa report the most significant new case and recovery increases.
2. The Americas show moderate case numbers.
3. Eastern Mediterranean and Western Pacific regions display comparatively lower new cases.
4. Recovery trends closely follow case trends, indicating active response efforts.

**Geographic Distribution**
The map visualizes the spread of cases across regions:
1. Highest density clusters in North America, Europe, and Asia.
2. Smaller clusters visible in Africa, South America, and Australia.
3. Regional clusters help identify concentration zones and possible hotspots.

**Insights**
1. Case volume, recovery trends, and health system response show strong regional correlation.
2. Europe and Africa currently exhibit the most recent case increases.
3. Recovery rates are positive relative to deaths, indicating successful treatment progression.
4. 6M active cases represent areas still under pandemic pressure.

**Use Cases**
This dashboard can assist with:

1. Monitoring pandemic trends by region.
2. Supporting policy and public health decisions.
3. Academic research and comparative analysis.
4. Reporting and awareness for stakeholders and government agencies.
